# Recommended usage

Ancestral nationalism is most suitable for historical nation-states that prioritise preserving the continuity, self-determination and identity of a long-established ancestral population.

Ancestral nationalism is most suitable for nation-states where preserving the continuity of the long-established ancestral population is considered a core political objective, alongside the preservation of the historical culture, heritage and identity associated with that population. It is best suited to historical nation-states with a strong sense of ancestral continuity and a population that sees descent itself, rather than civic values or cultural assimilation alone, as the defining basis of nationhood.

This model is most workable where the long-established ancestral population can be sufficiently defined in practical terms, such as through historical continuity, official records, legal descent rules or other mechanisms for establishing membership boundaries. The clearer and more administratively workable those boundaries are, the easier it becomes to implement the system consistently. Ancestral nationalism is therefore more likely to be viable in older, well-established nation-states with strong historical identity and continuity than in recently formed, highly mixed or historically settler societies.

Because ancestral nationalism does not extend full national membership to outsiders, it can in principle be more flexible on migration at the level of visas or temporary residency, since the long-established population retains stronger control over political membership and can reverse migration policy more easily than under civic or cultural nationalism. This makes it more attractive to those who want to retain self-determination, maintain stable national identity and culture, minimise external political influence, and reduce the long-term difficulty of reversing demographic change after citizenship has already been granted.

Ancestral nationalism is most oriented toward stable national identity, population continuity, self-determination and long-term preservation of the historical nation. It is therefore most suitable where these aims are given greater importance than political inclusiveness or open pathways to national membership for outsiders.
