# Genetic data collection methods

Genetic data collection methods measure DNA and produce markers or sequences. These laboratory methods provide the raw information used by later ancestry, lineage and kinship analyses. The service prices below reproduce those listed in the supplied documentation rather than updated quotations.



## **Autosomal single nucleotide polymorphism genotyping**

Autosomal SNP genotyping measures a predefined set of common single nucleotide polymorphisms across the autosomal genome. It often also includes sex-chromosome markers and sometimes mitochondrial markers. The result consists of genotype calls at hundreds of thousands or millions of selected positions. It is efficient for broad and moderate-resolution ancestry inference, but it tests selected markers rather than the full genome.



**Analyses it can support include:**

* Principal component analysis.
* Admixture analysis.
* Relative matching.
* Identity-by-descent detection.
* Local ancestry inference.
* Allele-frequency comparison.
* Some haplotype methods.

## **Whole-genome sequencing**

Whole-genome sequencing, or WGS, reads most or nearly all of the DNA sequence across the genome. Rather than examining only selected markers, it attempts to determine the sequence across nearly the whole genome. It provides the most comprehensive general-purpose data source described here. Its disadvantages are higher cost, greater data volume and more demanding computation.



**Analyses it can support include:**

Everything supported by SNP genotyping, often at higher resolution, together with:

* Rare-variant analysis.
* More detailed haplotype analysis.
* Denser local ancestry inference.
* Structural-variant analysis.
* More complete Y-chromosome and mitochondrial recovery.
* Better use of imputation reference data.
* Deeper research-grade analysis.



**Example consumer services:**

* [**Sequencing.com**](https://get.sequencing.com/shop-all-bundles/) — $399–$799.
* [**DNA Complete**](https://dnacomplete.com/tier-selection/) — $245–$1,295.
* [**Nucleus**](https://mynucleus.com/health/shop) — $787.



## **Whole-exome sequencing**

Whole-exome sequencing, or WES, reads mainly the exons: the protein-coding portions of genes. It generates sequence data from a defined subset of the genome. Although it contains variants useful for some ancestry work, it was designed primarily for medical genetics. It provides more detail than sparse marker sets in coding regions, but its coverage is uneven for ancestry analysis because it omits most of the genome.



**Analyses it can support include:**

* Some population-structure analysis.
* Some kinship inference.
* Some ancestry estimation.



## **Short tandem repeat testing**

Short tandem repeat testing, or STR testing, measures repeat lengths at selected genomic locations. It produces an STR profile. STRs are highly variable and mutate relatively quickly, making them useful for:

* Forensic identification.
* Paternity testing.
* Close kinship.
* Some paternal-line studies.

They are less suitable than genome-wide SNP or WGS data for broad ancestry inference.



**Analyses it can support include:**

* Parentage testing.
* Forensic matching.
* Close-relative comparison.
* Y-STR paternal-line comparison.



## **Y-chromosome testing**

Y-chromosome testing focuses on the Y chromosome through:

* Y-SNPs.
* Y-STRs.
* Full Y sequencing.

The Y chromosome is transmitted through the paternal line with limited recombination. This makes it informative for one direct male lineage, but it applies only to people with a Y chromosome and represents only one ancestral pathway.



**Analyses it can support include:**

* Paternal haplogroup assignment.
* Direct paternal-line comparison.
* Surname and paternal-line studies.
* Phylogenetic placement of male lineages.



**Example consumer service:**

* [**FamilyTreeDNA**](https://www.familytreedna.com/) — $119–$449.



## **Mitochondrial DNA testing**

Mitochondrial testing sequences or genotypes mitochondrial DNA.

Because mitochondrial DNA is inherited maternally, it can trace one direct maternal line across long periods.

Like Y-chromosome testing, it represents one lineage rather than the entirety of someone’s ancestry.



**Analyses it can support include:**

* Maternal haplogroup assignment.
* Direct maternal-line comparison.
* Maternal-line phylogeny.



**Example consumer service:**

* [**FamilyTreeDNA**](https://www.familytreedna.com/) — $159.



## **Methods for broad population testing**

Autosomal SNP genotyping is presented as the most practical method for broad population testing because it is comparatively inexpensive and scalable. Whole-genome sequencing can provide greater resolution, but its cost and processing requirements make it harder to use across an entire population. A combined approach could use:

* WGS for people with strong documentary evidence who may be suitable for the reference panel.
* SNP genotyping for routine population comparison.
* Higher-resolution testing where additional confidence is required.

Where cost and scale are the main constraints, SNP genotyping is the preferred starting point. Where maximum resolution is the priority, WGS is the stronger option.



## **Methods for direct lineage**

STR, Y-chromosome and mitochondrial testing can support direct lineage and kinship work. Whole-genome sequencing can add further information to strengthen subsequent analysis. The method should therefore be selected according to whether the question concerns broad population ancestry, a specific lineage or a close family relationship.
