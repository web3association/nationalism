# What is Nationalism?

Nationalism is an ideology or political movement that holds that a nation should form its own political community and have collective self-determination. A nation is a people who understand themselves as sharing a collective identity, which may be based on ancestry, territory, history, citizenship, culture, language, religion, political values or some combination of these.

National self-determination may take the form of an independent state, territorial autonomy, national unification or control over the institutions governing the nation. Nationalism generally promotes solidarity, loyalty and mutual obligations among members of the nation, often placing the nation’s interests and continuity above competing interests.

A nation is distinct from a state. A nation is a community of people with a collective identity and belonging, whereas a state is a set of political institutions exercising authority over a territory and population. A nation-state is a state that claims legitimacy as the political expression of a nation, although the boundaries of nations and states rarely correspond perfectly. Different forms of nationalism disagree over what defines the nation, who belongs to it and what rights or responsibilities national membership entails.



## **The goal of nationalism**

The central goal of nationalism is to secure the nation’s collective self-determination, political agency and continuity over time. In practical terms, this means ensuring that the nation has sufficient control over the institutions, laws and decisions that shape its future.

For nations without their own state, the goal with nationalism may involve independence, autonomy, unification or formal political recognition. For nations with an established state, it may involve protecting their sovereignty, maintaining national institutions, strengthening political cohesion and preserving the characteristics regarded as central to their national identity.



## Priorities for preservation and continuity

What nationalism seeks to preserve depends on how the nation is defined. Different forms of nationalism help with prioritising the preservation and continuity of different things:

* **Civic-state continuity** - Prioritises preserving the state’s sovereignty, shared institutions, laws, citizenship, civic values, constitutional order, systems of governance and political identity.
* **Cultural continuity** - Prioritises preserving the nation’s language, customs, traditions, beliefs, historical memory, social norms, public culture and way of life.
* **People continuity** - Prioritises preserving the nation as a distinct historical people, including its long-established ancestral population, descendants and inherited group identity.

These priorities can often be combined. For example, some nationalists may argue that preserving the continuity of a distinct people is also the best way to preserve that people’s culture and civic-state. In this view, demographic continuity supports cultural and civic-state continuity. Others may place greater emphasis on cultural continuity, arguing that the nation can endure even if its population changes, so long as newcomers adopt the language, customs, values and way of life. Civic nationalists, by contrast, may see civic-state continuity as the core requirement: the nation-state persists as long as its laws, institutions, citizenship and shared political values remain intact.

In this sense, civic-state continuity can be seen as the most minimal form of preservation, cultural continuity as a broader form, and people continuity as the most encompassing, because it aims to preserve not only the civic-state and culture, but also the historical population associated with them.



## **Three distinct forms of nationalism**

Different forms of nationalism define the nation in different ways and prioritise different forms of continuity and preservation, such as political continuity, cultural continuity or people continuity. The following are some distinct forms of nationalism that will be used for comparisons across this analysis on nationalism:

* **Civic nationalism** - Defines the nation through shared citizenship, laws, political institutions, civic values and participation in a common public life. It prioritises civic-state continuity, seeking to preserve the nation’s sovereignty, institutions, legal order, civic unity and equal citizenship.
* **Cultural nationalism** - Defines the nation through a shared culture, including language, customs, traditions, beliefs, social norms, historical memory and way of life. It prioritises cultural continuity, seeking to preserve and transmit the nation’s culture across generations, including through education, assimilation and common cultural practices.
* **Ancestral nationalism** - Defines the nation through continuity with a distinct historical people, including shared ancestry and lineage. It prioritises people continuity, seeking to preserve the long-established ancestral population associated with the nation. This is often as the basis for also preserving its culture and political inheritance as well.

## **Most important mechanisms of political membership**

The most defining components of political membership within a nation-state are concerned with which individuals receive citizenship and voting power and who can become a representative in national government.

* **Citizenship** - Determines who is formally recognised as belonging to the nation-state and who holds legal rights, protections and duties. Citizenship determines who has permanent membership within the nation.
* **Voting power** - Determines who can influence the direction, leadership and laws of the nation-state. Those with voting power help shape political control and the nation’s future.
* **Representation** - Determines who can govern, legislate and speak on behalf of the nation-state. Representation shapes whether political power is translated into governance that reflects the nation’s interests.



## **Multiracial and multicultural societies**

Nationalism does not necessarily require a nation-state to be ancestrally homogenous or monocultural. Each nation-state may adopt different rules for membership, residence and cultural expectations.

* **Civic nationalism** - Can be multiracial and multicultural in principle, because membership is based on citizenship and shared political institutions rather than ancestry. However, it may still require integration into a common civic culture.
* **Cultural nationalism** - Cultural nationalism can be multiracial if people of different ancestries adopt the national culture. It is often not multicultural as cultural nationalism will often mean giving priority to one national culture.
* **Ancestral nationalism** - Ancestral nationalism may tolerate cultural or racial diversity within the nation-state, but it defines full national membership through descent from the historic national population. Non-ancestral groups may be accepted as residents but they are not regarded as full members of the nation.

Nationalism is primarily concerned with defining who belongs to the nation and who holds the authority to shape its political future. Other groups and cultures may coexist within a nation-state under these different forms of nationalism, but the extent of that coexistence depends on how the nation defines membership, residence and cultural boundaries, which is then reflected in its migration, residency and integration policies.
