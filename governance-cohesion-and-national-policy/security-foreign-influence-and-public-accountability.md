# Security, foreign influence and public accountability

Security-sensitive decisions affect the independence, safety and survival of the state. They concern:

* Defence.
* Intelligence.
* Law enforcement.
* Borders.
* Cybersecurity.
* Foreign influence.
* Strategic assets.

These areas often involve sensitive information or powers that justify stronger vetting, citizenship requirements, conflict-of-interest rules and security clearance.



## **Defence and national security**

Relevant decisions include:

* Military policy.
* Defence spending.
* Conscription.
* Military leadership.
* Intelligence.
* Cybersecurity.
* Strategic planning.

**Civic nationalists** generally consider any citizen eligible to serve if they pass relevant checks and demonstrate loyalty. The central criteria concern conduct, competence and commitment to the state rather than ancestry.

**Cultural nationalists** may take a similar approach while expecting military and security institutions to reflect national values and traditions.

**Ancestral nationalists** may prefer sensitive roles to be held by the historical population. They may be concerned about foreign ties, divided loyalties, coercion involving relatives or communities abroad and external influence. Robust clearance and conflict-of-interest procedures remain particularly important.



## **Policing and internal security**

Internal security includes:

* Crime enforcement.
* Public order.
* Counter-extremism.
* Gang enforcement.
* Surveillance.
* Protest policing.
* Community safety.

**Civic nationalists** emphasise equal law enforcement and protection for citizens.

**Cultural nationalists** may regard policing as supporting public order, inherited norms and national expectations.

**Ancestral nationalists** may be concerned that non-ancestral participation in policing could introduce group favouritism or political pressure from particular communities.



## **Border security and immigration enforcement**

Border enforcement concerns:

* Border policing.
* Visa compliance.
* Asylum processing.
* Deportation rules for non-citizens.
* Customs.
* Unauthorised migration.

**Civic nationalists** may support strong enforcement alongside fair procedures and humanitarian obligations.

**Cultural nationalists** may regard border control as necessary to keep cultural integration manageable.

**Ancestral nationalists** may see it as essential for protecting demographic and political continuity.

The choice of whom to admit is considered in **Immigration policy: scale, selection and duration**. Enforcement concerns how those rules are implemented.



## **International relations and diplomacy**

Foreign-policy decisions include:

* Treaties.
* International agreements.
* Sanctions.
* Foreign aid.
* Tariffs.
* Trade arrangements.
* Export controls.
* Import restrictions.
* Customs enforcement.
* Diplomatic representation.

**Civic nationalists** seek benefits for the citizenry, prosperity and security. Citizens from different backgrounds can represent the state loyally.

**Cultural nationalists** may additionally expect diplomats and officials to represent national traditions and cultural interests.

**Ancestral nationalists** may worry that strong external ties could influence decisions in favour of other states. Possible safeguards include conflict-of-interest rules, foreign-funding restrictions, disclosure of external connections and security vetting.



## **Foreign ownership and strategic assets**

Ownership can affect sovereignty where it concerns:

* Land.
* Ports.
* Energy infrastructure.
* Media.
* Telecommunications.
* Critical minerals.
* Farmland.
* Defence industries.
* Major corporations.

**Civic nationalists** may restrict foreign ownership where it threatens security or democratic independence.

**Cultural nationalists** may be concerned about effects on local traditions, cultural industries and national autonomy.

**Ancestral nationalists** may want land, wealth, employment and strategic assets to remain under historical-population control rather than external groups or foreign-linked communities.



## **Foreign influence through communities**

States, parties, religious authorities and organisations can seek influence through diaspora communities. This may involve:

* Funding institutions.
* Supporting media.
* Political mobilisation.
* Monitoring dissidents.
* Pressure on families.
* Intimidation.
* Fundraising for foreign causes.

Such activity may influence domestic politics while also creating risks for residents who oppose the external authority.



**Diaspora attachments and public office**

Residents and citizens may retain family, emotional, economic or political connections abroad. These connections can persist across generations. They may become especially significant during diplomatic disputes, security crises or conflicts involving countries of origin. The document’s discussion of voting and representation identifies concerns that these connections may affect foreign aid, diplomacy or military decisions. Those arguments are presented in **Voting, representation and eligibility for public office**.



**Security-sensitive and ordinary employment**

Not every job raises the same level of risk. Sensitive positions may provide access to confidential information, strategic systems or consequential decisions. Other positions are primarily technical, routine or locally administrative. Distinguishing these categories allows a state to maintain safeguards without necessarily restricting ordinary opportunities needed for migrants to support themselves.



## **Administrative oversight**

Public accountability requires institutions that can identify misconduct, misuse of resources and improper influence. Relevant mechanisms include:

* Audits.
* Ethics rules.
* Freedom-of-information systems.
* Ombuds institutions.
* Procurement rules.
* Anti-corruption laws.

**Civic nationalists** prioritise impartial institutions, transparency, equal treatment and rule of law.

**Cultural nationalist**s may regard honesty, trust and low corruption as part of national civic culture.

**Ancestral nationalists** may be concerned about favouritism within external communities. However, transparent rules, independent oversight and individual accountability could provide sufficient safeguards.



## **Security, accountability and institutional design**

Security policy is not only about identifying potential threats. It also concerns how institutions remain competent, transparent and accountable while exercising sensitive powers. The appropriate arrangements may differ between military command, public office, technical infrastructure, policing and ordinary employment.

Different nationalist approaches place different weight on citizenship, cultural alignment and ancestry, but all must address the practical questions of vetting, external influence and misuse of public authority.
