# National culture

National culture covers a wide range of beliefs, practices, institutions and forms of expression. The following catalogue is not exhaustive. Its purpose is to show the different cultural aspects that a population may want to preserve and protect.



## **Core beliefs and values**

These are underlying ideas that shape how people think and behave:

* Shared values.
* Moral norms.
* Belief systems.
* Religious traditions.
* Ideas about right and wrong.
* Views on individualism and collectivism.
* Attitudes toward authority, fairness and justice.
* Attitudes toward freedom, equality, duty and responsibility.
* Attitudes toward time, change and tradition.
* Attitudes toward risk, success, competition and meritocracy.



## **Identity and collective meaning**

These give people a sense of belonging to the nation:

* National identity.
* Shared history.
* Collective memory.
* Mythologies, rituals, taboos and afterlife beliefs.
* National narratives.
* Patriotism.
* Symbols of belonging.
* Ideas about “who we are” as a people.
* Founding figures, national heroes and martyrs.
* National achievements, shared victories and traumas.
* Monuments and memorials.
* National mottos.
* Historical anniversaries.
* Stereotypes about national character.
* “Golden age” narratives.
* Stories of survival, sacrifice or resistance.
* Public remembrance practices.



## **Social organisation and everyday life**

These shape daily behaviour and social relationships:

* Family structures.
* Kinship patterns.
* Gender roles.
* Class and social hierarchy.
* Community expectations and obligations.
* Social etiquette.
* Manners.
* Hospitality norms.
* Friendship and relationship patterns.
* Child-rearing practices.
* Expectations around ageing and respect for elders.
* Marriage expectations.
* Dating norms.
* Household division of labour.
* Expectations of parents, children and siblings.
* The role of extended family.
* Neighbour relations.
* Social distance and personal space.
* Gift-giving customs.
* Visiting customs.
* Rules around punctuality.



## **Language and communication**

Culture can be strongly expressed through communication:

* National language or languages.
* Regional dialects.
* Minority languages.
* Communication styles.
* Greeting styles.
* Gestures, eye contact, facial expressions, touch and personal space.
* Formal and informal speech.
* Nonverbal communication.
* Storytelling traditions.
* Humour.
* Proverbs and sayings.
* Ways of expressing emotion or disagreement.
* Slang.
* Idioms.



## **Traditions, customs and ritual life**

These are repeated practices that express cultural meaning:

* Customs.
* Rituals.
* Ceremonies.
* Life-cycle events such as birth, marriage and funerals.
* Religious observances.
* Seasonal traditions.
* National holidays.
* Festivals.
* Commemorations.
* Everyday routines with symbolic importance.
* New Year rituals.
* Seasonal feasts.
* National day celebrations.
* Ritual clothing.



## **Arts and symbolic expression**

These reflect how a nation expresses beauty, meaning and identity:

* Literature.
* Poetry.
* Music.
* Dance.
* Theatre.
* Film.
* Visual arts.
* Sculpture.
* Crafts.
* Folk art.
* Fashion and traditional dress.
* Architecture.
* Design styles.



## **Food and material culture**

These include physical objects and practical expressions of culture:

* Cuisine.
* Eating habits.
* Food taboos.
* Meal structure.
* Cooking methods.
* Traditional dishes.
* Shared dining practices.
* Clothing.
* Housing styles.
* Tools and technologies.
* Interior decoration.
* Everyday objects with cultural significance.



## **Institutions and public life**

National culture is also shaped by formal structures:

* Education systems.
* Political culture.
* Legal traditions.
* Civic values.
* Government institutions.
* Military traditions.
* Public ceremonies.
* Media institutions.
* Religious institutions.
* Economic culture.
* Work ethic.
* Business norms.
* Volunteerism and civic participation.



## **Historical and geographic influences**

Culture develops in relation to place and past experience:

* Geography.
* Climate.
* Relationships to land and the environment.
* Migration history.
* Colonial history.
* Wars and conflicts.
* Trade and outside influences.
* Urban and rural life.
* Regional variation.
* Historical turning points.



## **Popular and contemporary culture**

These include modern and changing forms of shared culture:

* Popular music.
* Television.
* Film and streaming culture.
* Social media behaviour.
* Internet culture.
* Celebrity culture.
* Sports.
* Youth culture.
* Fashion trends.
* Consumer habits.
* Contemporary public debates.



## **Diversity within national culture**

National culture is rarely uniform. Differences may exist between:

* Regional subcultures.
* Ethnic and linguistic communities.
* Indigenous cultures.
* Religious minorities.
* Immigrant influences.
* Generations.
* Urban and rural communities.
* Social classes.
