# Nations, states and nation-states

Nationalism is an ideology or political movement that holds that a nation should form its own political community and have collective self-determination.

A nation is a people who understand themselves as sharing a collective identity. That identity may be based on ancestry, territory, history, citizenship, culture, language, religion, political values or some combination of these.



## **Nations and states**

A nation is distinct from a state.

* **A nation** is a community of people with a collective identity and sense of belonging.
* **A state** is a set of political institutions exercising authority over a territory and population.
* **A nation-state** is a state that claims legitimacy as the political expression of a nation.

The boundaries of nations and states rarely correspond perfectly. Different forms of nationalism disagree over what defines the nation, who belongs to it and what rights or responsibilities national membership entails.



## **National self-determination**

National self-determination may take the form of an independent state, territorial autonomy, national unification or control over the institutions governing the nation.

Nationalism generally promotes solidarity, loyalty and mutual obligations among members of the nation. It often places the nation’s interests and continuity above competing interests.

The central goal of nationalism is to secure the nation’s collective self-determination, political agency and continuity over time. In practical terms, this means ensuring that the nation has sufficient control over the institutions, laws and decisions that shape its future.

For nations without their own state, this may involve independence, autonomy, unification or formal political recognition.

For nations with an established state, it may involve protecting sovereignty, maintaining national institutions, strengthening political cohesion and preserving the characteristics regarded as central to national identity.



## **Political membership**

Some of the most defining components of political membership within a nation-state concern who receives citizenship and voting power, and who can become a representative in national government.

* **Citizenship** determines who is formally recognised as belonging to the nation-state and who holds legal rights, protections and duties. It determines who has permanent membership within the nation.
* **Voting power** determines who can influence the direction, leadership and laws of the nation-state. Those with voting power help shape political control and the nation’s future.
* **Representation** determines who can govern, legislate and speak on behalf of the nation-state. It shapes whether political power is translated into governance that reflects the nation’s interests.

National membership can also describe wider ideas about national identity and social belonging. Legal citizenship and social acceptance do not always coincide. A person may possess citizenship without being widely regarded as part of the nation, while a long-term resident may participate extensively in society without being a citizen.



## **National membership and residence**

Nationalism is primarily concerned with defining who belongs to the nation and who holds the authority to shape its political future.

This does not necessarily mean that everyone residing within a nation-state must belong to the national community on the same terms. Other groups and cultures may coexist within a state, depending on its rules for membership, residence and cultural expectations. Those boundaries are reflected in migration, residency, citizenship and integration policies.
